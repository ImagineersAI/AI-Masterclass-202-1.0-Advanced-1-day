How the day worksOne business tool, built in five steps.

You spend the day developing a complete business skill or plugin. Choose from 30 tools before the course, then build yours around three real examples from your work.

The skill or plugin is a folder of instructions, prompts, templates and a small program. You can use it with Claude or ChatGPT. Each step ends with a saved version you can return to.

  1. Step 1Write the prompt.Describe the job and see what your first instructions produce.Read why and what you will do

    Why this matters

    A short request leaves the AI to guess what matters. It may skip a business rule, make an assumption or give a different answer next time. Your tool needs the method an experienced person would follow.

    What you will do

    Write a prompt that describes the job, the input, the method and the output you need. Run it twice on the same input and compare the answers. Save this first version. Before coffee, ask the AI to try three real inputs and report where the prompt fails.

    What you will have

    A first working prompt, a saved version and examples of what needs to improve.

  2. Step 2Have the AI interview you and improve it.Find the rules and requirements you did not know you needed to explain.Read why and what you will do

    Why this matters

    You know your business, but your first prompt will leave things unsaid. The AI needs to know what you would reject, which rules matter and what evidence must support the result. An interview helps make those requirements explicit.

    What you will do

    Review the AI's results from three real inputs. Let it interview you about the gaps, then rewrite the prompt with your answers. Add a checklist for what must be checked before delivery, run the examples again and save version two.

    What you will have

    An improved prompt with your business rules, evidence requirements and a delivery checklist.

  3. Step 3Create the template.Give every result the same structure, so your team knows where to find things.Read why and what you will do

    Why this matters

    Even a good prompt can produce different headings, tables and layouts on different runs. A board paper or business report needs a familiar structure. Readers should be able to compare results without rebuilding the document each time.

    What you will do

    Turn your best result into an HTML template, a file that sets the page layout. Fix the headings, tables and places for evidence. Tell the AI to fill the template. Deliberately damage a saved version, then restore the earlier one.

    What you will have

    A reusable page template, plus the confidence to recover a version when a change goes wrong.

  4. Step 4Add the Python program.Let the AI explain the figures. Let a tested program calculate them.Read why and what you will do

    Why this matters

    A large language model (LLM) generates text from patterns learned during training. A convincing number in its answer does not prove a calculation was run. Models can solve maths and use calculators, but generated arithmetic can still be wrong. That is a problem in a board deck, a budget or a report someone must approve.

    What you will do

    Ask the AI to write a small Python program for sums, percentages, scores, dates and sorting. The program applies the same rules every run. For example, it calculates the percentage change before the AI writes the commentary. Check three results by hand and test missing values, zeroes and other difficult inputs. Code can contain mistakes too.

    What you will have

    Repeatable calculations with checks you can inspect. The AI uses those calculated figures in the report rather than inventing them as it writes.

  5. Step 5Test it and prepare it for handover.Prove that the tool works on difficult inputs and that someone else can run it.Read why and what you will do

    Why this matters

    One successful example is not enough. Your tool may fail on a missing field, a different date format or a document you have not tried. Your colleague also cannot use instructions that exist only in your head.

    What you will do

    Add a start-here file, sample inputs and tests with answers you know in advance. Ask a second AI to look for faults. Then hand the tool to a neighbour, give no help and note where they get stuck. Fix the problems, save the version and send a private copy to GitHub.

    What you will have

    A checked skill or plugin with instructions, examples and saved versions, ready for its first real job at work.

30 choices · One tool per delegateChoose the tool you will build.

Each tool is built by one delegate. Send us your first and second choice. Earlier bookings get first priority, and we will confirm your tool before the day. Everyone takes home every tool built by the group.

What every tool on this list has in common

  • It brings a skill you may not have, or saves hours. Each one carries an expert's method inside it.
  • It needs very little to start. One document, a few files or a page of your own notes is enough.
  • Something in it has to be exactly right. A small program does the sums, dates and checks, so the AI cannot get them wrong.

You already take home our finished tools, so they are not on this list. They are the Excel model builder, the board pack reader, the dashboard builder, the Gantt creator, the hand-drawn slide, the slide script builders, the explainer video and the issue tree.

Board and big decisions

1. Board paper writer.

A manager's rough notes and figures go in. A board paper comes out in your format: the decision sought, the options scored, the money table and the risks. The program adds up the money table and refuses to deliver if a figure in the text differs from the table.

2. Decision table with a flip test.

Your options, criteria and weights go in. A ranked table comes out. The program then tests every weight and reports the smallest change that would change the winner, which shows how safe the decision is.

3. Negotiation prep sheet.

A page of notes on the deal goes in. One page comes out with both sides' interests, your walk-away point and the range where a deal is possible. It also plans what to concede and in what order. The program values each concession in rands.

Money

4. Management accounts commentary.

One month's management accounts go in. One page comes out that explains every change above a set size against budget and last year, with a chart of what moved profit. The program works out every change, so the words cannot disagree with the numbers. This is the safe choice for anyone.

5. Investor update with runway.

A short table of the month's key figures and your notes go in. An investor update comes out in the standard order: highlights, problems, figures and requests. The program works out the monthly changes and how many months of cash are left.

6. Annual accounts red-flag reader.

One set of annual financial statements goes in. One page comes out with the key ratios and two warning scores that analysts use (Altman Z and Beneish M). Every flag carries the page number behind it. A board member or lender gets an analyst's first read in minutes.

7. Timesheet and bill from your week.

Your calendar export and a list of sent emails go in. A draft timesheet comes out by client, then invoice lines at the right rate for each client. The program rounds time to your billing units and applies the rates.

8. Fee and scope page.

Notes from a scoping call and your rate card go in. The scope, phases, hours, fees and payment dates come out. This is the manageable part of a proposal, and the part where mistakes cost money.

Deals and funding

9. First-look deal screen.

A teaser or information memorandum goes in. One page comes out: fit against your mandate, any automatic rejections, a rough returns sum and the ten questions to ask first.

10. Term sheet comparison with exit payouts.

Two term sheets go in. Every difference comes out side by side. The program works out who gets paid what at several sale prices, which is where the terms really differ.

11. Data room check.

The list of files in a data room and your due diligence checklist go in. One page comes out showing what is present, what is missing and the percentage complete by area, with a request list ready to send.

12. Impact report.

A small table of project results and targets goes in. A funder-ready page comes out: results against targets, cost per person reached and a diagram linking money to outcomes.

13. Funding application builder.

The funder's questions and your project notes go in. A drafted application comes out. The program holds every answer inside its word limit, checks that no question is skipped and makes the budget add up.

Bids and buying

14. Bid evaluation sheet.

The bids and the published criteria go in. A score for each bidder comes out with reasons. The program applies the 80/20 or 90/10 price formula and ranks the bidders.

15. Bid CVs in one format.

Staff CVs in any layout go in. CVs come out in the format the tender demands. The program works out years of experience from the dates and keeps each CV inside the page limit.

16. Request for quotation drafter.

A page of notes on what you need goes in. A request for quotation comes out with a clear specification, a reply form and the scoring sheet you will use on the replies.

17. Supplier quote comparison.

Three quotes go in. One page comes out comparing them on the same basis: tax, delivery, payment terms and warranty. The program works out the true cost over the life of the purchase.

People

18. CV shortlister.

A job description and a handful of CVs go in. A ranked shortlist comes out, with the evidence from each CV behind every score. The program applies your weights and removes anyone who fails a must-have.

19. Interview pack and panel score.

A job description and one CV go in. Questions aimed at the gaps in that CV come out, with a scoring sheet. After the interview, the program combines the panel's scores and shows where the panel disagreed.

20. Roster checker.

One week's or month's roster goes in. A list of rule breaks comes out, such as too little rest between shifts or too many nights in a row. The program also shows who carries more than a fair share of weekends and nights.

21. Organisation chart reader.

A list of names, job titles and managers goes in. A chart you can click through comes out. The program counts the layers and how many people report to each manager, and flags managers with too many or too few.

22. Procedure from a voice note.

Record yourself explaining a job for ten minutes. A written procedure comes out with numbered steps, a checklist, who is responsible for each step and a short quiz for staff. The program checks that every step has exactly one owner.

Operations and projects

23. Process map with the bottleneck.

A description of a process and rough times for each step go in. A diagram comes out showing who does what. The program finds the step that limits the whole process and the share of time that adds value.

24. Project money check.

A short task list with budget, spend and percentage complete goes in. One page comes out saying whether the project is ahead or behind for the money spent, and what it will cost at the finish. It uses the standard method that project managers call earned value.

25. WhatsApp group to action list.

One exported group chat goes in. One page comes out with the decisions made, the actions by person and the questions nobody answered. The program reads the dates and counts how long each request waited.

Documents

26. Contract dates and duties.

One contract goes in. Every duty and deadline comes out on one page and as a calendar file you can import. The program works out the real dates, such as the last day to give notice.

27. Confidentiality agreement triage.

One confidentiality agreement and your standard terms go in. A verdict comes out: sign, negotiate or refer to a lawyer, with the clauses behind it. This is the simplest tool on the list to build.

28. Case timeline.

A bundle of letters, emails and notes goes in. A dated timeline comes out with a source for every entry. The program sorts the dates, measures the gaps and flags entries that contradict each other.

29. Evidence brief with checked quotes.

Five to ten reports go in. A briefing note comes out with a quote and page number for each claim. The program checks that every quote appears word for word in its source, and refuses to deliver if one does not.

30. Claims checker.

One draft article or report goes in. A list comes out of every claim that can be checked and the source it needs. The program recalculates every percentage and total in the text.

Zed is where you will work. You choose Claude Agent or Codex inside Zed, and Zed installs that agent for you. Python still needs a separate installation. Zed has Git controls, but your AI also needs the git command to save versions. The checks below confirm it is available.

2. Set up your computer

Follow only the section for your computer. If a program is already installed and its check works, keep it.

Mac: install and check

Download and install Zed

  1. Open zed.dev/download and download the stable version for Mac.
  2. In Finder, open Downloads and double-click the downloaded .dmg file.
  3. Drag Zed into Applications. Open Applications, then double-click Zed. If macOS asks whether to open it, choose Open.
  4. Leave Zed open. You will connect your AI in step 3 below.

Install Git, Python and the GitHub helper

Press Command and Space, type Terminal, then press Return. This opens the window where you paste commands. Paste one command at a time and press Return.

First check whether Homebrew, the installer we use for these programs, is already there:

brew --version

If it prints a version, skip the Homebrew installation below. If it says command not found, paste:

/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"

Follow its prompts. Your Mac may ask for its login password; nothing appears while you type it. At the end, follow the installer's Next steps commands. They make Homebrew available in Terminal and differ between Intel and Apple Silicon Macs. Then run brew --version again.

Install Git to save versions, Python to run calculations, and the GitHub helper to connect your account:

brew install git python gh

If Homebrew says your macOS version is unsupported, send us that message before continuing.

Create your course folder and add the Excel reader

Use this folder in your home directory, outside OneDrive, iCloud and Dropbox:

mkdir -p ~/ai-masterclass
cd ~/ai-masterclass

Create a separate Python environment for your course tools:

python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install openpyxl

openpyxl lets your tools read and write Excel files. This installs it for your course folder without changing the Mac's other Python programs.

Check the installations

Run each command separately. Git should report git version, Python should report Python 3, and the GitHub helper should report gh version, each followed by numbers.

git --version
python3 --version

gh --version
.venv/bin/python -m pip show openpyxl

The last command should show Name: openpyxl and a version. If any command fails, send us the message. Then continue with step 3 below.

Windows: install and check

Download and install Zed

  1. Open zed.dev/download and download the stable Windows installer.
  2. Open Downloads in File Explorer and double-click the installer. Follow its prompts. If Windows asks for permission, check the installer is from Zed before accepting.
  3. Press the Windows key, type Zed and open it. Leave it open; you will connect your AI in step 3 below.

Install Git, Python and the GitHub helper

Press the Windows key, type PowerShell, then press Enter. Paste one command at a time and press Enter. These commands use Windows' installer, called WinGet. Accept its installation prompts.

winget install -e --id Git.Git
winget install -e --id GitHub.cli

Install Python 3.13, the version used for this setup guide:

winget install -e --id Python.Python.3.13

If winget is not recognised, open Microsoft Store, find App Installer by Microsoft Corporation, and install or update it. Close PowerShell, open it again, then retry.

When the installations finish, close PowerShell and open a fresh window so it can find the new programs.

Create your course folder and add the Excel reader

Create the folder in your user directory, outside OneDrive, iCloud and Dropbox:

New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force "$HOME\ai-masterclass"
Set-Location "$HOME\ai-masterclass"

Create a separate Python environment for your course tools and install the Excel reader:

py -3.13 -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install openpyxl

You do not need to change PowerShell's script permissions. The command runs Python directly.

Check the installations

Run each command separately. Git should report git version, Python should report Python 3.13, and the GitHub helper should report gh version, each followed by numbers.

git --version
py -3.13 --version

gh --version
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip show openpyxl

The last command should show Name: openpyxl and a version. If any command fails, send us the message. Then continue with step 3 below.

3. Connect your AI inside Zed

  1. Close Zed and open it again so it can find the programs you just installed.
  2. Choose File → Open Folder, then open ai-masterclass inside your home or user folder.
  3. Open Zed's command search: Command, Shift and P on Mac, or Control, Shift and P on Windows. Type zed: acp registry and select it.
  4. Install Claude Agent for your Claude subscription, or Codex for your ChatGPT subscription. Zed downloads the selected agent. If you have both subscriptions, you may install both.
  5. Open the Agent Panel using the agent icon in Zed's status bar. Use its new-thread menu to choose the agent you installed.
  6. For Claude Agent, type /login in that thread and choose the Claude Code sign-in option. For Codex, choose Sign in with ChatGPT when prompted. Complete the sign-in in your browser, then return to Zed.
  7. Type Say hello and send it. Check that your chosen agent answers.

Use the subscription sign-in route. You do not need to buy separate API credits for this course setup. If your company account blocks the sign-in, ask its administrator to enable access before the day.

4. Connect GitHub and do the final check

In the Terminal or PowerShell window you used earlier, tell Git your name and GitHub email. Replace the examples inside the quotes with your own details:

git config --global user.name "Your Name"
git config --global user.email "you@example.com"

Connect the GitHub helper:

gh auth login --web --git-protocol https

If asked whether to authenticate Git, choose yes. Follow the sign-in instructions. You may see a short code and a browser page where you enter it.

Check the connection:

gh auth status

You should see a message confirming that you are logged in to github.com as your username.

Finally, paste this request into your Claude Agent or Codex thread in Zed:

Check this laptop for the course. Report the Git and Python versions, whether gh is signed in to GitHub, and whether openpyxl is installed in this folder's .venv. Do not install or change anything. Tell me what passed and what needs fixing.

You are ready when the agent answers and all four checks pass. Send us your GitHub username if you have not already done so.

If a check fails

Send us a screenshot of the failed step and its message, without passwords or sign-in codes. We will help you before the day. Doors open at 08:00 for any remaining laptop problems.
